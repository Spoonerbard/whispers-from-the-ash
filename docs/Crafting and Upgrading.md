#rulesofplay 

**I. Core Mechanic: Modular Crafting**

The heart of this system is **modular crafting**. Instead of finding complete magic items, players will often discover components, blueprints, or recipes that can be combined and upgraded to create unique and powerful gear.

**A. Component Types:**

1. **Base Components:** These are the foundations of items. Examples:
    - **Repeating Crossbow Frame:** The base for a repeating crossbow.
    - **Enchanted Blade Core:** The core of a magical sword or axe.
    - **Wand Conduit:** The central piece of a wand or staff.
    - **Reinforced Armor Plating:** The foundation for a suit of magical armor.
    - **Living Wood:** Can be used for any of the above and counts as both plant and wood, as well as the base material.
2. **Upgrade Components:** These enhance the base components, adding special properties or improving existing ones. Examples:
    - **Clockwork Repeater Mechanism:** Adds extra shots to a repeating crossbow.
    - **Croydonite Focusing Lens:** Allows a wand to channel more powerful spells, but with risks.
    - **Shadow-Touched Thread:** Grants a cloak the ability to enhance stealth.
    - **Mourning-Infused Metal:** Adds necrotic damage to a weapon, but might corrupt the wielder.
    - **Fey-Touched Silk:** Adds psychic damage to ranged attacks that use it.
    - **Beholder Eye Lens:** Adds a random beholder eye effect to each shot.
    - **Elemental Essence:** Adds elemental damage to a weapon.
3. **Enchantment Recipes/Blueprints:** These are instructions for combining specific components to create a particular magical effect.
    - **Blueprint: Everbright Lantern:** Combines a lantern frame with a light crystal and a specific type of oil.
    - **Recipe: Potion of Spider Climb:** Requires specific herbs, monster parts, and a refined [[Croydonite]] catalyst.
    - **Schematic: Croydonite-Powered Blade:** Details how to combine a blade core, [[Croydonite]] shards, and a power conduit.

**B. Finding Components:**

- **Exploration:** Components can be found throughout [[the Mournland]]]:
    - Hidden in ruins.
    - Guarded by monsters.
    - Inside of dungeons and ancient structures.
    - Offered as rewards for completing quests.
    - Extracted from defeated enemies (e.g., a special mechanism from a clockwork construct).
- **Salvage:** Players might be able to salvage components from broken or unwanted magic items. This could be a risky process, potentially destroying the item or yielding only a small amount of usable material. This is less effective than using unidentified items to create magical essence with Troxler, but it is an alternative.
- **Purchase:** As Sanctuary grows, some basic components might become available for purchase, but rarer components will almost always need to be found through exploration.

**II. Crafting with Troxler (and other Artisans)**

- **Troxler's Expertise:** Troxler is the primary NPC for modular crafting, but as Sanctuary grows, other skilled artisans with different specializations might become available.
- **Tier-Based Crafting:** Troxler's ability to craft with certain components or blueprints will be tied to his current tier (Apprentice, Journeyman, Expert, Master). He won't be able to work with advanced components or complex blueprints until he gains more experience.
- **Crafting Process:**
    1. **Presentation:** The party presents Troxler (or another artisan) with the desired base component, upgrade components, and any necessary blueprints or recipes.
    2. **Cost Calculation:** Troxler calculates the cost of the crafting process, based on the complexity of the item, the rarity of the components, and his own skill level. The cost will usually involve gold and might also require Magical Essence or other rare materials.
    3. **Crafting Time:** Crafting takes time, ranging from a few hours for simple upgrades to several days or even weeks for complex items.
    4. **Potential Complications:** Introduce an element of risk, especially when working with volatile materials like Croydonite. You could have Troxler make a crafting check, with a chance of failure or a complication arising (e.g., the item is cursed, the item has a reduced effect, the item breaks after one use, etc.).
- **Experimentation:** As Troxler gains experience (and reaches higher tiers), he might be willing to experiment with new combinations of components or try to create entirely new items based on the party's ideas. This could lead to unique and powerful creations but also carries a higher risk of failure.

**III. Example: Upgrading the Repeating Crossbow**

Let's follow the example of the repeating crossbow:

- **Tier 1 (Apprentice):**
    - **Base Component:** The party finds a **Repeating Crossbow Frame** in a ruined armory. It's a martial weapon that allows the wielder to fire two bolts as an attack, dealing 1d6 piercing damage per bolt.
    - **Upgrade Component:** They also find a **Clockwork Repeater Mechanism** inside a damaged clockwork soldier.
    - **Crafting:** Troxler can attach the mechanism to the frame, increasing the number of bolts fired to three.
- **Tier 2 (Journeyman):**
    - **Upgrade Component:** The party discovers a **Fey-Touched Silk** after defeating a group of corrupted fey creatures.
    - **Crafting:** Troxler can incorporate this into the crossbow, adding 1d4 psychic damage to each bolt.
    - **Blueprint:** The party finds a **Blueprint: Accurate Repeater** in a hidden cache.
    - **Crafting:** Troxler can use this blueprint to modify the crossbow, granting it a +1 bonus to attack rolls.
- **Tier 3 (Expert):**
    - **Upgrade Component:** The party obtains a **Beholder Eye Lens** from a dangerous encounter with a mutated beholder.
    - **Crafting:** Troxler can carefully integrate the lens into the crossbow. Now, after the crossbow fires its normal bolts each round it can fire an additional effect determined by rolling on a table similar to a beholder's eye rays, scaling the DC to be appropriate.
    - **Upgrade Component:** The party refines raw [[Croydonite]] into **Stable [[Croydonite]] Bolts**.
    - **Crafting:** Troxler can craft these bolts for the crossbow. They deal extra fire damage but have a chance of exploding prematurely.
- **Tier 4 (Master):**
    - **Experimentation:** Troxler, inspired by the party's discoveries, experiments with combining various components, potentially creating a truly unique and legendary repeating crossbow with multiple enchantments and unpredictable effects. He might even be able to incorporate the Singing Stones from the Sky Song Tavern into the crossbow, giving it the ability to cast spells or produce other musical effects.

**IV. Progression and Customization**

- **Item Tiers:** You can create tiers for crafted items, just like you have tiers for Troxler and the town. For example:
    - **Tier 1 (Basic):** Simple upgrades, +1 weapons, common magic item effects.
    - **Tier 2 (Enhanced):** More powerful upgrades, +2 weapons, uncommon magic item effects.
    - **Tier 3 (Masterwork):** Significant enchantments, +3 weapons, rare magic item effects.
    - **Tier 4 (Legendary):** Unique and powerful effects, potentially even creating new artifact-level items.
- **Specialization:** Players can choose to specialize in certain types of items, becoming known for their unique creations. For example, one character might focus on crafting powerful crossbows, while another might specialize in creating enchanted armor or powerful wands.
- **Player Agency:** Give players a lot of freedom in choosing how to upgrade their items. Encourage them to experiment with different combinations of components and to come up with their own ideas for new creations.
- **Balance:** Carefully balance the power of crafted items. While they should be rewarding, they shouldn't overshadow other aspects of the game or make the characters too powerful too quickly.

**V. Expanding the System**

- **Other Artisans:** As Sanctuary grows, other artisans could set up shop, offering different crafting specialties (e.g., a master armorsmith, an expert alchemist, a skilled enchanter).
- **Crafting Downtime:** Crafting could be a major downtime activity, allowing players to create new items or upgrade existing ones between expeditions.
- **Crafting Quests:** Introduce quests specifically related to crafting, such as finding rare materials, recovering lost blueprints, or learning new techniques from master artisans in other parts of the world.
- **Rival Artisans:** Introduce rival artisans who compete with Troxler (and the party) for resources and prestige. This could lead to interesting conflicts or opportunities for sabotage.

By implementing this modular crafting system, you create a dynamic and engaging way for players to acquire and customize magic items. You give them agency in shaping their characters' development, and you tie the crafting process directly to exploration and the growth of Sanctuary. The possibilities are endless, and you can tailor the system to fit your specific campaign and the unique flavor of [[the Mournland]].
