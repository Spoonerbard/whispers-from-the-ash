
# **Session Two Recap: Water, Water Everywhere**

The air hung heavy with the dust of the Mournlands, a stark contrast to the twisted, unnatural landscape that lay just beyond the rickety palisade marking the edge of Salvation. This frontier town, a fragile beacon of hope and civilization, clung stubbornly to existence on the border of Cyre, the once-proud kingdom swallowed whole by the cataclysmic Mourning a century ago. Within its makeshift walls, a party of three adventurers, already seasoned by their trials in this blighted region, were about to welcome two new faces into their ranks.

Talon, the shifter, joined the group with an air of... well, not _mystery_, exactly. More like a guy trying to look mysterious while failing spectacularly. He said he was "bored" and wanted to explore the deadly Mournlands, but his explanation was about as convincing as Homer Simpson claiming he didn't eat the last donut. He _tried_ to seem intense, but mostly came off as a guy who might trip over his own feet while trying to sneak. The other adventurers probably figured he was hiding something, or maybe he just really, _really_ needed a change of scenery. Maybe both.

Secondly, a surprisingly chipper kobold by the name of Gronkie bounced into their midst. A bard with eyes full of wanderlust and a set of well-worn bagpipes strapped to their back, Gronkie's ambition burned as brightly as the strange mists that perpetually swirled within the Mournlands. Their stated goal was simple, if a tad unusual: to brave the horrors of the fallen kingdom, gather tales of its lost glory and present horrors, and most importantly, earn enough coin to finally realize their lifelong dream - opening a bagpipe shop. One could only imagine the cacophony of sound that would emanate from such an establishment, but Gronkie seemed unfazed by the potential auditory assault, their spirit fueled by the prospect of untold stories and a future filled with the drone of their beloved instrument.

Regardless of their true motivations, both Gronkie and Talon dutifully signed the adventuring license, a somber document that served as both permission and a grim reminder of the dangers that awaited them. With their signatures etched onto the parchment, they were officially bound to the group, their fates now intertwined with those of the three already hardened by their time in the Deadlands. Sheriff Karr, his gaze lingering a little longer on Talon, offered a curt nod, a silent acknowledgment that their presence was accepted, if not entirely trusted.

With their paperwork begrudgingly approved by Sheriff Karr and the ink still drying on the adventurer's license, Gronkie and Talon were directed towards their assigned lodging – Shack B. It was hardly the lap of luxury, likely more akin to a dilapidated shed than a comfortable dwelling, but in Salvation, any shelter from the harsh elements and the lurking dangers of the Deadlands was a welcome refuge.

Inside Shack B, they found the three adventurers they'd be joining: Raymar, Gyda, and Rayleigh, the veterans who had already braved the edges of the Mournlands. The introductions within Shack B were likely brief, perhaps even perfunctory. Seasoned adventurers in a place like Salvation wouldn't have time for lengthy pleasantries. A nod, a shared glance, a brief exchange of names – these were the currencies of camaraderie in a land where trust was hard-earned and easily broken. 

The newly formed party, barely settled into the cramped confines of Shack B, were jolted awake by a deafening **BOOM!** Dust rained down from the shack's rickety ceiling as the ground trembled beneath them. Before they could even fully grasp what had happened, a figure burst through the doorway, wreathed in smoke and smelling faintly of burnt ozone. This was Troxler, the gnome artificer and tinker, her wild hair singed and her goggles askew. Known throughout Salvation for her eccentric experiments and explosive inventions. Troxler, with a frantic energy that bordered on manic, launched into an explanation. She had been investigating a bizarre phenomenon concerning a previous adventuring party's ill-fated foray into the Mournlands. Apparently, two of the party members, during a particularly harrowing battle, had consumed standard healing potions only to suffer a horrifying side effect: they were instantly regressed to the age of childhood!

Troxler, her eyes gleaming with a mixture of excitement and concern, explained her theory. She believed the ambient magical energies permeating the Mournlands were corrupting mundane magical items, causing unpredictable and often dangerous effects. The healing potions, brewed with ingredients from outside the affected area, had reacted violently with the corrupted magic, resulting in the age regression.

Her proposed solution, and the reason for her explosive interruption, was to create a new type of healing potion, one specifically attuned to the Mournlands' twisted magic. To do this, she needed a crucial ingredient: **contaminated water from the Mournlands itself.**

Troxler thrust a specially crafted flask towards the group. It shimmered with arcane runes, a testament to the gnome's ingenuity. This, she explained, was a magical containment flask, designed to safely hold the corrupted water without triggering any unwanted reactions. She tasked the party with venturing into the Mournlands, specifically to a lake located to the northwest, and retrieving a sample of its tainted water. Only then could she begin brewing her experimental potions, potentially creating a lifeline for adventurers braving the dangers of the fallen kingdom.

While the others were still reeling from Troxler's explosive entrance, Talon, in a rare moment of competence that didn't quite align with his "bored" persona, headed straight to Silas at the general store. He grabbed supplies to make a stretcher, a sewing kit, and ordered a copy of "When There Are No Clerics" - basically, a field medic manual for dummies. The book wouldn't arrive until later, but the fact that he even thought to order it suggested that maybe, just maybe, there was more to Talon than met the eye. Or maybe he just panicked. It was hard to tell with him.

Talon returned to Shack B, arms full of medical gear, to find things had gotten complicated. Silas, the store manager (and secret Mournlands explorer, apparently), wanted them to clear out a ghoul-infested lightning rail station to the north. He offered gold and a discount, which was tempting. Then, Meadowlight, the town's overworked cleric, showed up. She was stuck babysitting two adventurers who'd been turned into kids by bad potions, and their original party was missing in the northwest. She begged the group to find them. Suddenly, their simple water-fetching mission had become a lot more complex.

So, within the cramped confines of Shack B, the party now faced a trifecta of perilous quests, each pulling them in a different direction:

1. **The Northwest:** Search for the missing adventuring party, a mission of mercy fueled by Meadowlight's plea and the hope of reversing the age regression. This aligned with Troxler's need for a water sample from the lake, also located in the northwest.
2. **The North:** Clear out the ghoul and husk-infested lightning rail station, a dangerous task offered by the secretive Silas, with the promise of gold and a valuable discount.
3. **The Northwest (again):** Specifically, Stillwater Lake to collect the tainted water sample for Troxler's experiments, a crucial step in potentially creating safe healing potions for use within the Mournlands.

Faced with a trio of daunting tasks, the party ultimately decided to prioritize the collection of the tainted water sample from Stillwater Lake. They opted to leave Troxler's crafted magical containment flask behind in Salvation, perhaps deeming it too cumbersome for the journey. 

Reaching the crater floor, they found themselves enveloped in the unnatural fog that perpetually shrouded the Mournlands. However, as they approached Stillwater Lake, the fog thinned, revealing a scene of eerie desolation. Before them lay the still, dark waters, and upon its surface, the ghostly silhouettes of shipwrecks emerged from the gloom. Two vessels were identifiable: a riverboat named the "Sovereign's Pride" and a smaller, unnamed cargo vessel, both resting at unnatural angles, half-submerged in the tainted water.

As they approached, Talon, ever vigilant, scanned the wreckage. But even his keen eyes failed to spot the danger lurking on the deck of the "Sovereign's Pride." Suddenly, a searing blast of eldritch energy erupted from the riverboat, narrowly missing Talon. It was then they saw them: magical turrets, remnants of Cyre's advanced magical technology, now twisted and corrupted by the Mourning, had targeted them.

The peaceful lakeside erupted in chaos as the party was attacked by makeshift turrets - repurposed wizard staves, now firing blasts of eldritch energy. While the others returned fire, using the wrecked ships for cover, Talon and Rayleigh hid behind the cargo vessel. In a moment of questionable judgment, Talon peered into the water, and promptly came face-to-face with a giant, mutated goldfish with a shark-like maw and a venomous tongue.

Before Talon could become lunch, Gronkie put the monstrous fish to sleep with a spell, and Rayleigh yanked him back. But the close call triggered a surge of wild magic. Talon gained resistance to all damage, while Rayleigh, caught in the surge, began draining the life force from both Talon and the now-stirring goldfish. The magical backlash left Talon weakened but gave Rayleigh a boost, turning him into an unlikely life-force battery.

After a tough fight, the party finally disabled the last of the repurposed-staff turrets. But as darkness fell, they were forced to take shelter in the wreckage of the "Sovereign's Pride," surrounded by the dangers of the Mournlands and a very angry, probably still mutated, goldfish. Their mission to collect the water sample was a bust, and leaving the special flask behind now seemed incredibly foolish. To make matters worse, while Talon was relatively safe in the hold thanks to his new magical resistance, the others were exposed to a sudden downpour of acid rain. Things had officially gone from bad to worse aboard the "Sovereign's Pride."

This wasn't ordinary rain, but a caustic downpour, each drop sizzling as it struck the wreckage and its occupants. Raymar, Gyda, and Gronkie, still reeling from the battle with the turrets and the encounter with the monstrous goldfish, were caught in the open, vulnerable to the corrosive precipitation. The effects were immediate and bizarre.

The acid rain unleashed another wave of wild magic, transforming both Rayleigh and Gronkie into toads. They were forced to take shelter in the hull of the sovereign's pride, where they found a collection of skeletons that had been stained or painted red. The once-feared assassin and the cheerful bard were now slimy amphibians, their gear clattering uselessly to the deck. With two toads in tow, the party abandoned their mission as dawn broke, trudging back to Salvation in defeat. The wild magic surge at the lake, a consequence of their actions, had summoned a cluster of flumphs, which now followed the party, drawn to the lingering magical energy.

The party's return to Salvation was met with stunned silence, then pity. Troxler would be disappointed, Meadowlight worried, and Silas, no doubt, was observing from the shadows, his own plans disrupted. What should have been a triumphant return was instead a croaking, flumph-accompanied mess. Amidst this, Raymar, the Spore Druid, was lost in her own world. Still high from the lingering effects of a confusion spell, amplified by the corrupted lake water and the wild magic, she was utterly bewildered, experiencing a reality far removed from the grim scene around her. The Mournlands had won this round.

Lost in her magic-induced haze, Raymar stared at a flumph, convinced she understood its silent language. She "conversed" with it, babbling a mix of druidic lore and nonsense. Suddenly, her eyes widened, and she announced a prophecy received from the flumph: **"The sanction shall shatter the earth and the works of man shall be undone!"** Was this a true vision from the heart of the Mournlands, or just the ramblings of a druid on a very bad trip? Nobody was sure, especially since they were still dealing with the two party members who had been turned into toads.
