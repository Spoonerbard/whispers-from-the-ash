# **Encounter: The Displaced Depot**

**Location:** The Silent Grove (Forest Region), north of the cliff face that marks the edge of the Mournland.

**The Hook:**

- **Rumor:** "Old Man" Silas, the shopkeeper, or one of the more knowledgeable deputies (perhaps Dal, trying to impress the party) mentions the old lightning rail line that used to run through this region before the Mourning. He tells a story about a station that was known to be located several miles to the east, but some folks claim to have seen it in the Silent Grove recently. "It's like the whole damn building got up and walked," he might say. He's also heard tell of people going missing searching for the station, and warns the party to be careful.
- **Conflicting Information:** The party might find conflicting information about the station's location on old maps or in historical records. This discrepancy adds to the mystery.
- **Personal Connection:** Perhaps one of the party members has a personal connection to the lightning rail or to someone who worked on it before the Mourning. This could add emotional weight to the encounter.

**The Scene:**

- **The Silent Grove:** The forest is eerily silent, with petrified trees and an unnatural stillness in the air. The color coding of magic suggests transmutation magic is strong here, and perhaps there's a hint of chronomancy magic as well (black, associated with the time distortion).
- **The Station:** The party stumbles upon the ruins of a lightning rail station, seemingly plucked from its original location and dropped into the middle of the forest. It's a single-story structure made of stone and metal, bearing the distinctive architectural style of the lightning rail. The building is partially overgrown with strange, mutated vegetation. There are signs of recent disturbance around the station, such as footprints and trampled plants. Tracks lead in and out of the station, but they seem to disappear or reappear at odd angles.
- **Out of Place:** The station is clearly out of place. There are no tracks leading to or from it, and the surrounding terrain doesn't match the descriptions of its original location. It is also facing the wrong direction, suggesting that it was moved here unceremoniously.
- **Signs of a Struggle:** There are signs of a struggle inside the station – overturned furniture, broken equipment, and maybe even some dried bloodstains or drag marks. There are also signs of recent habitation, suggesting that someone or something has been using the station as a base.

**The Mystery:**

- **How did it move?:** The primary mystery is how the station moved from its original location to the Silent Grove. This could be due to:
    - **Time Distortion:** A localized time anomaly caused by the Mourning, which shifted the station through time and space.
    - **Reality Warping:** The Mourning's influence has warped the fabric of reality, causing the station to be displaced.
    - **Powerful Magic:** A powerful entity or a group of individuals (e.g., the Order of the Broken Sky, the Disciples of the Unwritten Name, or even the Lord of Blades) might have moved the station for their own purposes.
    - **The Entity Beneath the Glass Plateau:** The entity imprisoned beneath the Glass Plateau might have caused this as a display of its power.
- **Who was here?:** The party needs to determine who was using the station before them and what happened to them. Were they explorers, researchers, bandits, or something else entirely?
- **What are the dangers?:** The station itself might be dangerous, due to residual magic, unstable energy, or creatures that have taken up residence.

**Encounters:**

- **Conductor Husks:** The station is infested with Conductor Husks (the zombie commuters we designed earlier). They are animated by the lingering necromantic energy (yellow) and the distorted chronomancy magic (black) of the area. There could be a particularly powerful Conductor Captain among them, perhaps a former stationmaster.
- **The "Warped" Wildlife:** The area around the station could be inhabited by creatures warped by the Mourning's influence. These could be mutated versions of ordinary animals or entirely new, bizarre creations. These creatures are hostile and territorial. Ideas include:
    - **Phase Wasp Swarm:** A swarm of wasps that can phase in and out of reality, making them difficult to hit. They have a venomous sting that causes hallucinations. Use **Swarm of Wasps** stats, but they can phase in and out of reality, and the poison causes hallucinations.
    - **Displacement Squirrels:** Small, furry creatures that can teleport short distances, making them difficult to track and attack. They are skittish and will flee if threatened. Use **Cat** stats but give them the ability to teleport up to 10 feet as a bonus action.
    - **Echo Owls:** Owls that can record and playback sounds, creating confusing and disorienting echoes. They are also able to mimic the voices of their victims, luring others into traps. Use **Owl** stats but give them the ability to record and playback sounds, and the ability to mimic voices.
- **The Remnants of an Expedition:** The party might find the remains of a previous group of explorers who came to the station but didn't make it out. This could be a recent expedition, or it could be a group that disappeared years ago. The remains could offer clues about the dangers of the station or the fate of its previous inhabitants. They might also have left behind valuable equipment or notes.
- **A Rival Faction:** The party might encounter agents of the Order of the Broken Sky, House Cannith, House Tharashk, or another faction, also investigating the station. This could lead to a tense standoff, a negotiation, or even a fight.

**Challenges:**

- **Navigating the Silence:** The unnatural silence of the grove can be disorienting and make it difficult to detect approaching threats.
- **Dealing with the Husks:** The Conductor Husks are a relentless and potentially overwhelming threat, especially in the confined space of the station.
- **Unstable Magic:** The station might be permeated with unstable magic, causing wild magic surges, spell distortions, or other unpredictable effects. The station could be considered to be under the effects of a permanent _hallow_ spell, but the effects are random and unpredictable.
- **Traps:** The station might contain traps, either left behind by its original occupants or set by whoever was using it more recently.
- **The Mystery Itself:** Piecing together what happened at the station and why it moved will require careful investigation, deduction, and potentially the use of divination magic.

**Rewards:**

- **Clues about the Mourning:** The station's displacement and the strange magic within it could offer valuable clues about the nature of the Mourning and the forces that caused it. The party might find records or logs that shed light on the events of that day.
- **Lost Technology:** The station might contain remnants of pre-Mourning lightning rail technology, potentially including components that could be used to repair or upgrade existing items. They might also find a damaged but potentially repairable lightning rail car.
- **Magical Artifacts:** The station could house a powerful magical artifact, either related to the lightning rail or to the magic that caused its displacement. This could be a valuable item, or it could be a dangerous object that needs to be contained or destroyed.
- **Information:** The party might find information about the activities of other factions in the Mournland, such as the Order of the Broken Sky, House Cannith, or the Steel Stoners. They might also learn more about the previous inhabitants of the station and their fate.
- **Safe Haven:** If the party can clear out the station and make it safe, it could serve as a temporary base of operations in the Mournland, offering shelter from the elements and a place to rest (though long rests would still be risky).

**Possible Developments:**

- **The Station Moves Again:** The station might suddenly shift locations again while the party is inside, transporting them to a different part of the Mournland or even to another plane of existence. This could be a recurring event, making the station a highly unpredictable and dangerous location.
- **The Lord of Blades' Interest:** The Lord of Blades might be interested in the station, either for its technology or for its connection to the reality-bending magic of the Mourning. He might send his forces to claim the station, leading to a confrontation with the party.
- **The Order's Intervention:** The Order of the Broken Sky might arrive at the station, seeking to study the magic that caused its displacement. They might offer to help the party, or they might try to take control of the station for themselves.

**The Displaced Depot is a compelling encounter that combines combat, exploration, and investigation. It introduces the players to the strange and unpredictable nature of the Mournland, provides clues about the larger mysteries of the setting, and offers the potential for significant rewards. Remember to emphasize the eerie atmosphere of the Silent Grove, the unsettling presence of the Conductor Husks, and the mystery surrounding the station's displacement. Let the players' choices and actions shape the outcome of the encounter and determine the fate of the Displaced Depot.**


# **Transmutation Vines: The Twisted Veins of the Mournland**

**Appearance:**

- **Metallic Growth:** These are not organic vines but rather metallic growths that have sprouted from the shattered remnants of the lightning rail tracks. They are a twisted, gnarled mess of metal, resembling a fusion of thorny vines and rusted cables.
- **Color Coding:** The vines are primarily a dull, rusted brown (the color associated with transmutation magic), but they might have streaks of other colors depending on the types of magic that have infused them. For instance, they might have patches of sickly yellow where they've come into contact with necromantic energy, or veins of blue where they've absorbed abjuration magic. They could have sparks of red running through them, suggesting that they are infused with evocation magic.
- **Movement:** The vines are not static. They writhe and twist slowly, even when there's no wind. They seem to reach out, almost as if they are searching for something. They will often move to block the path of the players, or even try to grab them. They are slow moving, but relentless.
- **Thorns and Barbs:** The vines are covered in sharp thorns and barbs made of twisted metal, making them dangerous to touch. These could be regular thorns, or they could be shaped like old railway spikes.
- **Croydonite Growths:** In some areas, the vines might have sprouted small, jagged growths of Croydonite, adding to their danger. These would glow with the colour associated with the magic of the area.

**Behavior and Abilities:**

- **Semi-Sentient:** The vines are not truly intelligent but possess a rudimentary, instinctual awareness, driven by the corrupted transmutation magic that animates them. They react to movement, sound, and especially to magic.
- **Growth and Spread:** The vines are slowly spreading throughout the area, growing from the broken rail lines and reaching out to engulf anything in their path. They are particularly attracted to sources of magic, and will try to "consume" them. They are also attracted to sources of metal, and will try to "consume" them as well. They seem to actively avoid areas with high concentrations of Croydonite, almost as if they are afraid of it.
- **Grasping Vines:** The vines can lash out and attempt to grapple creatures that come too close. They are surprisingly strong and difficult to break.
- **Constricting Coils:** If they successfully grapple a target, they will try to constrict it, crushing the life out of it. They might also try to drag their victims towards areas with higher concentrations of Croydonite or other hazards.
- **Metallic Thorns:** The thorns on the vines can pierce flesh and armor, potentially injecting a magical toxin or causing a debilitating effect related to transmutation magic.
- **Adaptive Resistance:** The vines might adapt to the types of damage they are exposed to, becoming resistant to certain elements or types of attacks after being struck by them. For example, if the party uses fire against the vines, they might develop a resistance to fire damage.

**Environmental Hazard:**

- **Difficult Terrain:** Areas infested with the vines are considered difficult terrain. The vines might also be hidden beneath the undergrowth, making it easy to stumble into them.
- **Entanglement:** Characters moving through areas with the vines risk becoming entangled. This could require a Strength or Dexterity check to escape, with failure resulting in the restrained condition.
- **Constriction:** Entangled creatures might take bludgeoning damage each round as the vines constrict them. The vines might also try to drag their victims towards more dangerous areas.
- **Magical Toxin:** The thorns on the vines could inject a magical toxin that causes temporary transmutation effects on a failed Constitution saving throw. Examples include:
    - **Polymorph Sickness:** The target's body begins to distort and change, causing disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls.
    - **Metallic Skin:** The target's skin becomes hard and metallic, granting resistance to physical damage but reducing their Dexterity.
    - **Uncontrolled Growth:** Parts of the target's body might begin to grow uncontrollably, causing pain and hindering movement.
- **Croydonite Exposure:** Vines growing near Croydonite deposits might be infused with the material, causing additional effects on contact (e.g., psychic damage, mutations, or levels of exhaustion).

(blind beyond this radius), passive Perception 6 **Languages:** — **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP)

**Traits:**

- **False Appearance:** While the Transmutation Vine remains motionless, it is indistinguishable from a normal, albeit metallic, উদ্ভিদ.
- **Adaptive Resistance:** When the Transmutation Vine takes damage from a specific type of energy (acid, cold, fire, lightning, or thunder), it gains resistance to that type of damage until the end of its next turn.
- **Magic Sense:** The Transmutation Vine can sense the presence of magic within 30 feet of it. It is instinctively drawn to sources of magic.

**Actions:**

- **Grasping Vine:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 7 (2d4 + 2) bludgeoning damage, and the target is grappled (escape DC 12). Until this grapple ends, the target is restrained, and the Transmutation Vine can't use its Grasping Vine attack on another target.
- **Constricting Coil:** One creature grappled by the Transmutation Vine must make a DC 12 Strength saving throw. On a failed save, the target takes 9 (2d6 + 2) bludgeoning damage and is pulled up to 5 feet towards the vine.
- **Thorn Lash:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 5 (1d6 + 2) piercing damage plus 3 (1d6) poison damage. The target must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution saving throw or suffer a random minor transmutation effect for 1 minute (e.g., their skin changes color, one of their limbs shrinks or grows, they develop a strange metallic sheen).

**Tactics:**

- **Grapple and Constrict:** The vines will prioritize grappling targets and then constricting them.
- **Drag to Danger:** They will try to drag grappled creatures towards areas with Croydonite or other hazards.
- **Coordinate Attacks:** If multiple vines are present, they will coordinate their attacks, attempting to entangle and overwhelm their enemies.

**Encounter Design:**

- **The Infested Station:** The Displaced Depot is overrun with Transmutation Vines. They might be coiled around the building, blocking entrances, or even animating parts of the station itself (e.g., a section of track that tries to grab the party).
- **The Rail Yard:** The area around the station could be a maze of twisted tracks and overturned rail cars, now infested with the vines. The party might have to navigate this dangerous terrain while avoiding the grasping tendrils.
- **The Guardian Vine:** A particularly large and powerful Transmutation Vine, perhaps animated by a Croydonite-infused heartwood or the spirit of a creature killed by the vines, could serve as a mini-boss encounter. This vine might have additional abilities, such as the ability to animate nearby metal objects or to regenerate lost hit points.
- **Puzzle Element:** The party might need to find a way to clear the vines without resorting to brute force. Perhaps there's a specific type of magic or a particular substance that can destroy them, or maybe they need to find a way to deactivate the source of the vines' animation.

**Rewards:**

- **Croydonite:** The vines might contain small amounts of Croydonite, which can be harvested (carefully) after the vines are destroyed.
- **Salvaged Metal:** The vines themselves are made of metal and could be salvaged for crafting or sold for scrap.
- **Rare Components:** The heartwood of a particularly large or powerful vine might be a valuable alchemical ingredient or a component for crafting a magic item related to transmutation.
- **Knowledge:** Studying the vines could reveal more about the nature of the Mourning's magic, the effects of Croydonite exposure, or the process of transmutation gone awry.

**The Transmutation Vines add a dynamic and dangerous element to the environment, turning the exploration of the Displaced Depot into a challenging and memorable encounter. They are a visual representation of the Mourning's corrupting influence and a tangible threat that the players will need to overcome. Remember to describe their movements and attacks in a way that emphasizes their metallic nature and their unsettling, semi-sentient behavior. Good luck, and may your players survive the twisted embrace of the Transmutation Vines!**
