#rulesofplay 


**I. Basic Needs (DMG p. 111)**

- **Food:** A character needs 1 pound of food per day.
- **Water:** A character needs 1 gallon of water per day, or 2 gallons in hot weather.
- **The DMG states:**
    - A character can go without food for 3 + their Constitution modifier days before suffering exhaustion. After that, they automatically gain one level of exhaustion per day.
    - A character who drinks only half the required amount of water must make a Constitution saving throw (DC 15) at the end of the day or suffer a level of exhaustion.
    - A character with no food or water automatically suffers a level of exhaustion each day.
    - Levels of exhaustion due to lack of food or water cannot be removed until the character eats and drinks the required amount.

**II.[[mournland]] Specifics**

- **Hot and Dry:** [[the Mournland]] is a wasteland. Assume characters need 2 gallons of water per day due to the harsh conditions. You can adjust this based on specific locations or weather events (e.g., sandstorms might increase water needs even further).
- **Scarce Resources:** Finding food and clean water in [[the Mournland]] is difficult. Natural sources are rare and often contaminated.
- **Foraging:**
    - **DC:** Increase the DC for foraging checks (DMG p. 111) in [[the Mournland]] to reflect the scarcity of resources. A base DC of 15 or even 20 might be appropriate, depending on the area.
    - **Contamination:** Foraged food or water might be contaminated. Have characters make a Constitution saving throw (DC 10 + the number of rations gathered this way) after consuming foraged goods. On a failed save, they suffer the poisoned condition for 1d4 hours. You could also introduce more severe consequences, like disease or mutations, for particularly tainted resources.
- **Spoilage:** Food spoils faster in [[the Mournland]] due to the heat, strange magical energies, or other environmental factors.
    - **Normal Rations:** Standard rations last for only half their normal shelf life in [[the Mournland]] (or less in particularly harsh areas).
    - **Perishable Food:** Unpreserved food (e.g., fresh meat, fruit) might spoil within hours in [[the Mournland]].

**III. Carrying Capacity and Encumbrance (DMG p. 176)**

- **Variant Encumbrance:** Strongly consider using the variant encumbrance rule. This will make players think carefully about how much food, water, and gear they carry.
- **Weight of Food and Water:**
    - 1 pound of food weighs 1 pound.
    - 1 gallon of water weighs about 8 pounds.
- **Containers:**
    - **Waterskins:** Hold about half a gallon of water (4 pounds).
    - **Food Pouches/Sacks:** Can hold a few pounds of food, depending on size.
- **Large Water Containers:** If players want to carry more water, they might need to invest in larger containers like barrels or specially crafted, reinforced waterskins. These would be heavy and potentially awkward to transport.

**IV. Guarding Supplies**

- **Theft:** Desperate creatures or opportunistic bandits might try to steal the party's food and water, especially if they are traveling through more populated areas of [[the Mournland]] (ruined settlements, etc.).
- **Contamination:** Enemies might attempt to poison or contaminate the party's supplies.
- **Spills and Damage:** Accidents, rough terrain, or attacks could damage containers, leading to loss of food or water. Especially water, which is hard to transport in large quantities.
- **Protection:**
    - **Watches:** Encourage players to set watches, not just to avoid ambushes but also to protect their supplies.
    - **Secure Storage:** Using sturdy, lockable containers can deter theft and protect against some types of damage.
    - **Spells:** Spells like _alarm_, _Leomund's tiny hut_, and _glyph of warding_ can be used to protect supplies.

**V. Crafting and Innovations**

- **Troxler's Role:** Troxler (or another skilled craftsman) could develop specialized gear to help with resource management:
    - **Reinforced Waterskins:** More durable waterskins that are less likely to be damaged or leak.
    - **Insulated Containers:** Containers that help keep food fresh for longer in the harsh environment.
    - **Water Purification Tablets:** Magical or alchemical tablets that can purify contaminated water, making it safe to drink.
    - **"Mourning Rations":** Specially prepared rations that are more compact, nutritious, and resistant to spoilage than standard rations. They might be made from unusual ingredients found in [[the Mournland]] and could have minor side effects.
- **Player Ingenuity:** Encourage players to come up with their own solutions for preserving and protecting their supplies. They might try to:
    - **Build traps** around their campsite to deter thieves.
    - **Use magic** to create food or water (e.g., _create food and water_ spell).
    - **Develop techniques** for extracting water from cacti or other desert plants.
    - **Train animals** to carry supplies or guard the camp.

**VI. Narrative Consequences**

- **Dehydration and Starvation:** Make the effects of dehydration and starvation felt. Don't just track exhaustion levels; describe the physical and mental toll these conditions take on the characters.
- **Difficult Choices:** Running low on supplies should force players to make difficult choices:
    - Do they push on to their destination, hoping to find more resources?
    - Do they turn back to Sanctuary, admitting defeat?
    - Do they take risks to acquire food and water, potentially exposing themselves to danger?
- **Moral Dilemmas:** Will the party share their dwindling supplies with those in need, or will they hoard them for themselves? Will they resort to stealing or violence to survive?

**Example Scenario:**

The party is three days' journey from Sanctuary, exploring a ruined city in [[the Mournland]]. They have limited water left, and their rations are starting to spoil. They discover a hidden cache of supplies left behind by a previous expedition, but it's guarded by a group of mutated creatures.

- **Choices:**
    - **Fight the creatures:** Risky, as they might be injured or expend more resources (spell slots, etc.).
    - **Try to sneak past:** Difficult, as the creatures are alert and have a keen sense of smell.
    - **Negotiate:** Perhaps the creatures are intelligent enough to be reasoned with or bribed (unlikely, but players might try it!).
    - **Abandon the supplies:** Continue exploring, hoping to find another source of food and water, or turn back to Sanctuary.
