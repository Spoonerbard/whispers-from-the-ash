**A. The Hook: What is West Marches D&D?**

- **Explain the core concept:**
    - "Imagine a D&D campaign where the players decide where they want to go, when they want to go, and who they want to go with. That's the heart of a West Marches game."
    - "Instead of a fixed group playing every week, we'll have a larger pool of players and each session is organized like an expedition into the unknown. You'll sign up for sessions that fit your schedule."
- **Highlight the appeal:**
    - **Flexibility:** "Perfect for people with busy schedules. You play when you can, without feeling like you're holding the group back."
    - **Player Agency:** "You're in the driver's seat. Explore the parts of the world that intrigue you."
    - **Emergent Storytelling:** "The stories we tell will come from your choices and discoveries, not a pre-written plot."
    - **Shared World:** "Even though different groups will explore, everything happens in the same world. Your actions can impact other players and the overall narrative."

**B. Setting the Stage - Brief World Overview**

- **Keep it concise:** Avoid overwhelming them with lore dumps. Offer a "movie trailer" version of the setting.
    - "Our game is set in the [Name of Region], a recently rediscovered wilderness, full of uncharted ruins, strange creatures, and forgotten magic."
    - "The only safe haven is the frontier town of [Town Name], where adventurers gather to share rumors, gear up, and plan their next expedition."
- **Tease some mysteries:**
    - "What happened to the civilization that built those ruins?"
    - "What are the legends surrounding the [Name of Landmark/Creature]?"
    - "What treasures might be found in the depths of the [Geographic Feature]?"
- **Avoid being too specific with what exists where.** Preserve that sense of discovery.

**C. Character Creation Guidance**

- **Simple starting point:**
    - Recommend everyone start at level [1-3]. Levels can be variable for each expedition, but that will be established beforehand for the player's benefit.
    - Suggest using standard array or point-buy for ability scores to keep things balanced.
    - The starting equipment of each class is fine for beginners.
- **Open to concepts:** Encourage them to think about their character's personality, motivations, and background, but keep it loose. The West Marches experience is great for character development.
- **Connection to the town:** Each character should have a reason to be in [Town Name] and a reason to explore the wilderness. (Greed, glory, knowledge, revenge, etc.)

**II. The First Session - Town Life & First Foray**

**A. Starting in Town**

- **Introduce [Town Name]:** Describe the sights, sounds, and important NPCs (shopkeepers, tavern owners, town guard, etc.).
- **Rumor Mill:** Present some rumors (true, false, and exaggerated) to spark interest in potential expeditions. "I heard there was a dragon in the mountains" "Be careful of the spiders in the forest, I hear they spit acid".
- **Player Introductions:** Have the characters introduce themselves and explain why they're here.

**B. Planning the First Expedition**

- **Guide them through the process:**
    - **Choose a destination:** Based on rumors, character goals, or player curiosity.
    - **Gather information:** Talk to NPCs, research in the town's library (if it has one), etc.
    - **Recruit party members:** (If this is the first session ever, it might just be the initial group, but explain how it normally works).
    - **Stock up on supplies:** (Emphasize the importance of preparation).
- **Don't railroad them:** Let them make their own choices, even if it seems like a "bad" idea. Learning from mistakes is part of the fun!

**C. A Short, Introductory Adventure**

- **Keep it simple:** A small dungeon, a nearby monster lair, a straightforward investigation.
- **Focus on the basics:** Teach them the core mechanics (skill checks, attack rolls, saving throws) in a low-stakes environment.
- **Emphasize exploration and discovery:** Hidden rooms, environmental storytelling, interesting loot.
- **End on a cliffhanger or with a new lead:** Encourage them to come back for more!
- **Make sure the session ends with the party returning to town.** This is crucial to the West Marches style.

**III. After the First Session - Maintaining Momentum**

**A. Session Recaps**

- **Encourage players to write them:** Each group should chronicle their adventures (from their character's perspective, if possible).
- **Post them publicly:** This creates a shared history and allows players to learn from each other's experiences.
- **Highlight consequences:** Did they clear out a monster nest? Did they anger a powerful faction? Make sure these events are reflected in future sessions.

**B. Downtime Activities**

- **Explain downtime options:** Crafting, training, research, carousing, etc. (Use the rules in the PHB or Xanathar's Guide to Everything).
- **Encourage players to pursue personal goals:** This adds depth to their characters and creates new plot hooks.

**C. Evolving World**

- **Introduce new rumors and events:** The world should feel alive and reactive to the players' actions.
- **Add new locations to the map:** As players explore, expand the known world.
- **Don't be afraid to change things:** If players drastically alter the landscape or the power dynamics, roll with it!
