This is a clever idea! Using the 5:30 PM hard stop as an in-game mechanic tied to food and water is a great way to manage session time without feeling overly restrictive. It provides a narrative justification for ending the session and naturally encourages players to consider their resources and time management. Here's a breakdown of the practical considerations, potential issues, and how to make it work effectively:

**Practical Considerations and Benefits:**

- **Hard Stop Enforcement:** It provides a clear, in-game reason for ending the session at 5:30 PM. It's no longer the GM arbitrarily stopping the game; it's the characters reaching their physical limits.
- **Time Management:** It encourages players to think about how they spend their time during the session, both in and out of character. They'll need to weigh the risks and rewards of exploring further versus returning to town before their supplies run low. This adds a layer of strategic decision making.
- **Narrative Flavor:** It reinforces the harsh realities of the Mournland, where resources are scarce and survival is a daily struggle. It emphasizes that even the most basic needs can become major challenges.
- **Reduced Bookkeeping:** You're right that aggressively tracking every pound of food and every gallon of water can be tedious. This system simplifies things while still retaining the thematic element of resource management.
- **Flexibility:** You can adjust the "depletion rate" of their supplies to match the planned content of the session. If you have a shorter session planned, you can narrate that they find less water on their return journey.

**Potential Issues and Solutions:**

- **Player Agency:** While you don't want to be too restrictive, you also need to avoid making the system meaningless.
    - **Solution:** Establish clear guidelines from the start. Explain the mechanic to your players and emphasize that it's a way to manage time and create a sense of realism. Provide in-game ways to extend their time, such as finding an oasis or successfully foraging.
- **Predictability:** If the system is too rigid, players might feel like they're just going through the motions, knowing exactly when the session will end.
    - **Solution:** Introduce some variability. You could have a hidden timer that's not always exactly 3.5 hours. Perhaps roll a d6 and subtract that many minutes from the end time to represent fluctuations in their supplies or unexpected delays. You can also narrate descriptions that make it clear that the supplies are running low and that they need to make a decision about whether to press on or return.
- **Forced Endings:** The system could lead to anticlimactic endings if the party is in the middle of something important when the timer runs out.
    - **Solution:** Give the players warnings as the deadline approaches. You can use descriptions like, "You estimate you have about an hour of supplies left" or "The sun is getting low, and your water skins are feeling light." You can also give them an in-game way to determine how long they can safely stay out such as a successful Survival check. If they are in the middle of a combat encounter, you could rule that they have enough supplies to finish the encounter but not enough to continue exploring afterward. You can also adjust the pacing of the session to try to reach a natural stopping point before the hard stop.

**Implementation Tips:**

- **Communicate Clearly:** Explain the system to your players upfront so they understand how it works and why it's in place.
- **Narrative Justification:** Tie the system to the story. Perhaps the characters are using special, lightweight, but expensive, rations that only last for a certain amount of time. Or maybe their water bladders are prone to leaking in the harsh Mournland environment. You could rule that it takes roughly 3 hours to travel between a safe haven and an adjacent square.
- **In-Game Warnings:** Provide in-game cues that their supplies are running low, giving them time to react and make decisions.
- **Flexibility:** Be prepared to adjust the system based on player feedback and the specific circumstances of the game.
- **Rewards for Efficiency:** Consider rewarding players who manage their time and resources effectively. For example, if they return to town with time to spare, they might have extra time for downtime activities or receive a small bonus for their next expedition.
- **The "One Last Thing" Rule:** To avoid abrupt endings, consider implementing a "one last thing" rule. When the players decide to head back to town, allow them to do one last quick action or exploration before the session ends, even if it technically pushes them past the deadline. This allows for a little more closure.

**Alternative Approaches:**

- **Session-Based Supplies:** Instead of tying supplies directly to real-world time, you could give each party a set amount of "exploration points" or "supply points" per session. Different actions (traveling, exploring, getting into combat) would cost a certain number of points. This provides a similar mechanic but abstracts it further from real-world time.
- **Time Trackers:** You could use a visual time tracker, like a clock or a series of tokens, to represent the passage of time and the depletion of supplies. This can help players visualize the time they have left and make more informed decisions.

**In conclusion, using the session's hard stop as a narrative element tied to food and water is a creative and effective way to manage time in your West Marches campaign. By communicating clearly with your players, providing in-game warnings, and being flexible with the system, you can create a sense of urgency and realism without sacrificing player agency. This will encourage players to be strategic, manage their resources effectively, and make the most of their time in the Mournland.**
