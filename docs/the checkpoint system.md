Here's how we can implement this system:

**I. Establishing Checkpoints:**

- **Requirements:**
    - **Clearance:** The party must first clear the chosen location of any hostile creatures or significant hazards (e.g., Conductor Husks, Transmutation Vines, dangerous traps).
    - **Securing:** They need to secure the area against the elements and potential threats. This might involve:
        - **Repairs:** Repairing damaged walls, roofs, or doors to provide shelter from storms and environmental hazards. This could require materials like wood, stone, or salvaged metal, as well as tools and skill checks (e.g., Carpentry, Masonry).
        - **Fortifications:** Building barricades, reinforcing entrances, or setting traps to deter creatures or other intruders. This could involve Survival, Investigation, or even Thieves' Tools checks to create effective defenses.
        - **Warding:** Using magic or special materials (like the Singing Stones or purified Croydonite) to create magical wards that protect the area from certain types of creatures or the corrupting influence of the Mournland. This might require Arcana checks or the assistance of NPCs like Troxler or a spellcaster from the Order of the Broken Sky.
    - **Time:** Establishing a checkpoint should take time, possibly several days or even weeks of in-game time, depending on the size and complexity of the location and the resources available.
    - **Cost:** The party might need to invest resources (gold, materials, favors) to secure the location. They might need to purchase supplies from Salvation, hire workers (if any are available), or trade for specialized tools or materials.
- **Benefits:**
    - **Safe Haven:** A secured checkpoint provides a safe place to take long rests, restoring hit points, spell slots, and other resources. The security of the location determines how safe it is. A well-fortified and warded checkpoint might eliminate the chance of interruptions entirely, while a more basic shelter might just reduce the frequency or severity of encounters.
    - **Storage:** Checkpoints can be used to store supplies, equipment, and treasures found in the Mournland, reducing the need to constantly carry everything back to Salvation.
    - **Crafting and Research:** Depending on the facilities available, checkpoints could be used for crafting, research, or other downtime activities. A checkpoint might have a workshop, a library, or even a laboratory, allowing players to create or upgrade items, study the Mournland's magic, or develop new technologies.
    - **Fast Travel:** As the network of checkpoints expands, it could create a form of fast travel within the Mournland, allowing the party to move more quickly between secured locations. This could be represented by reduced travel time or the ability to bypass certain hazards.
    - **Outposts:** Checkpoints could serve as bases for further exploration, allowing the party to delve deeper into the Mournland without having to return to Salvation after each expedition.

**II. Checkpoint Tiers:**

Similar to the town tiers, you can create different tiers for checkpoints, reflecting their level of security, facilities, and overall usefulness:

- **Tier 1: Basic Shelter:**
    - **Description:** A partially ruined building, a cave with a fortified entrance, or a makeshift camp with basic defenses.
    - **Benefits:** Provides shelter from the elements, reduces the chance of long rest interruptions (but doesn't eliminate it), allows for the recovery of hit points and offers a small storage space.
    - **Requirements:** Minimal repairs and fortifications.
- **Tier 2: Secure Outpost:**
    - **Description:** A more fortified location, such as a sturdy building with reinforced walls and doors, a cave with multiple chambers and a concealed entrance, or a well-defended camp with traps and alarm systems.
    - **Benefits:** Offers greater protection from the elements and intruders, significantly reduces the chance of long rest interruptions, allows for hit dice recovery on a long rest, and provides more storage space. Might include basic crafting facilities (e.g., a workbench, a forge).
    - **Requirements:** More extensive repairs and fortifications, potentially requiring specialized materials or skills.
- **Tier 3: Fortified Base:**
    - **Description:** A well-defended and self-sufficient base of operations, such as a restored section of the Displaced Depot, a fortified cave complex, or a small keep built from salvaged materials.
    - **Benefits:** Provides a high level of security, eliminates (or nearly eliminates) the chance of long rest interruptions, allows spell slot recovery on a long rest, and offers ample storage space. Includes more advanced crafting facilities, potentially a library or a laboratory. Might even have a magical power source or a connection to a ley line.
    - **Requirements:** Significant investment of time, resources, and effort. Might require the assistance of skilled NPCs, the recovery of rare materials, or the completion of specific quests.

**III. Challenges and Complications:**

- **Maintaining Security:** Checkpoints might be attacked by creatures of the Mournland, rival factions, or even bandits seeking to claim the location for themselves. The party might need to defend their checkpoints or even retake them if they are overrun.
- **Resource Depletion:** Maintaining a checkpoint requires resources, such as food, water, and materials for repairs. The party might need to regularly resupply their checkpoints or find ways to make them more self-sufficient.
- **Corruption:** The Mournland's influence could slowly corrupt a checkpoint, requiring the party to perform cleansing rituals, reinforce wards, or find ways to counteract the negative effects.
- **Unexpected Events:** The unstable nature of the Mournland could lead to unexpected events at checkpoints, such as sudden storms, reality distortions, or the appearance of strange creatures or phenomena.

**IV. Connecting Checkpoints to the Wider World:**

- **Faction Influence:** Different factions might try to influence or control the party's checkpoints. House Tharashk might offer resources and support in exchange for a share of any discoveries. The Order of the Broken Sky might seek to establish research outposts at these locations. House Cannith might be interested in any pre-Mourning technology found at the sites.
- **Trade Routes:** As the network of checkpoints expands, it could create new trade routes within the Mournland, connecting Salvation to other settlements or resources.
- **Settlement Growth:** Particularly successful or well-defended checkpoints could attract settlers, eventually growing into new outposts or even small towns. This could lead to new opportunities and challenges for the party, as they become responsible for the safety and well-being of a growing community.

**V. Examples of Checkpoint Locations:**

- **The Displaced Depot:** As we discussed, the cleared-out rail station offers a ready-made structure with the potential for expansion and defense.
- **The Sky Song Tavern:** If the party manages to fully clear and secure the tavern, it could become a valuable checkpoint, especially if they can restore some of its magical functions.
- **The Weeping Oasis:** While dangerous, the oasis offers a source of water, and if the party can deal with the corrupted nature of the place, it could become a viable outpost.
- **The Silent Grove:** A fortified area within the petrified forest could serve as a hidden and defensible location.
- **Caves in the Jagged Peaks:** Natural cave systems could be expanded and fortified, creating a secure and easily defensible base.

**By allowing the players to establish and upgrade checkpoints, you give them a tangible sense of progress and a greater stake in the world. You also create new opportunities for adventure, as they must defend their outposts, manage their resources, and deal with the challenges of establishing a foothold in the dangerous and unpredictable Mournland. Remember to tailor the specific challenges and rewards to your players' level and to tie the checkpoints to the larger themes and mysteries of your campaign. This will make the exploration of the Mournland even more rewarding and engaging.**
