#rulesofplay 



A hex crawl is a style of play where the game world is mapped out on a grid of hexagons (hexes), and the focus is on exploration and discovery. Each hex represents a specific area with its own terrain, encounters, and secrets. Players navigate the map hex by hex, uncovering the world's mysteries as they go.

**Key Components of a Hex Crawl:**

1. **The Hex Map:**
    
    - **Hex Grid:** The game world is divided into a grid of hexagons. Each hex represents a certain amount of distance (e.g., 6 miles, 12 miles, 24 miles).
    - **Terrain:** Each hex is assigned a terrain type (e.g., forest, mountain, desert, swamp). This affects travel time, encounters, and resources.
    - **Keyed Locations:** Some hexes are "keyed" with specific locations, encounters, or points of interest (e.g., a dungeon, a village, a monster lair, a mysterious ruin). These are the things players are meant to discover.
    - **Hidden Information:** Initially, most of the hex map is unknown to the players. They start with limited knowledge and must explore to reveal more.
2. **Exploration Procedures:**
    
    - **Movement:** Players decide which direction they want to travel and move from one hex to another.
    - **Travel Time:** The time it takes to travel through a hex depends on the terrain and the party's mode of transportation (e.g., on foot, on horseback, by boat).
    - **Determining Contents:** When the party enters a new hex, the Game Master (GM) determines what, if anything, is in that hex. This can be done in a few ways:
        - **Pre-Planned:** The GM has already decided what is in each hex beforehand.
        - **Random Encounters:** The GM rolls on a random encounter table tailored to the terrain type.
        - **A Mix of Both:** The GM has pre-planned some hexes with specific locations or encounters but uses random tables to fill in others or to add unexpected events.
    - **Discovering Locations:** When the party enters a hex with a keyed location, the GM describes it to the players. The players can then choose to investigate further.
3. **Resource Management:**
    
    - **Supplies:** Players need to track their food, water, and other supplies. Running out of supplies can have serious consequences.
    - **Time:** Time is a resource. Certain events might be time-sensitive, and spending too long in one area could have repercussions.
    - **Mapping:** Players are usually responsible for mapping their progress, either by drawing their own map or by filling in a blank hex map provided by the GM. Accurate mapping is crucial for finding their way back to known locations and for planning future expeditions.

**Why Use a Hex Crawl?**

- **Emphasis on Exploration:** Hex crawls put the focus squarely on exploration and discovery. The world feels vast and mysterious, and players are rewarded for venturing into the unknown.
- **Player Agency:** Players have a lot of freedom to choose where they go and what they do. They are not railroaded along a predetermined path.
- **Emergent Gameplay:** The combination of random encounters, pre-planned locations, and player choices creates a dynamic and unpredictable experience.
- **Sandbox Feel:** Hex crawls are great for creating a sandbox-style game where players can set their own goals and pursue their own interests.
- **Easy to Design:** Hex crawls can be easier to design than other styles of play because the GM can create content on a hex-by-hex basis, rather than having to plan out an entire storyline in advance.

**Hex Crawls vs. Point Crawls:**

It's worth noting the difference between hex crawls and point crawls, which are another exploration-focused style of play.

- **Hex Crawl:** Focuses on exploring a map divided into a grid of hexes. Movement and exploration are granular.
- **Point Crawl:** Focuses on moving between specific points of interest (locations) connected by paths. Movement is more abstract, and the space between locations is less defined.

Think of it this way: a hex crawl is like exploring a wilderness with a map and compass, while a point crawl is like traveling between cities on a road network.

**Hex Crawls in Your West Marches Campaign:**

Hex crawls are a perfect fit for the West Marches style because:

- **Decentralized Play:** The hex map provides a framework for multiple groups of players to explore the same world independently.
- **Persistent World:** Actions in one hex can have consequences in other hexes, creating a sense of a living, breathing world.
- **Exploration Focus:** The emphasis on exploration aligns with the West Marches philosophy of player-driven discovery.

**Tips for Running a Hex Crawl:**

- **Start Small:** Don't feel like you need to create a massive hex map right away. Start with a smaller area and expand as needed.
- **Use a Hex Map:** You can find blank hex maps online or use a hex-mapping software tool.
- **Create Interesting Locations:** Come up with unique and memorable locations to populate your hexes.
- **Develop Random Encounter Tables:** Tailor your tables to the different terrain types in your game world.
- **Make Travel Meaningful:** Don't just handwave travel time. Use it as an opportunity to introduce challenges, encounters, and resource management decisions.
- **Reward Exploration:** Make sure players are rewarded for exploring new areas and discovering new things.
