**Frequency and Severity:**

- **Roll for Hazards:** You can roll on this table at the start of each night the party spends outside a safe haven, or more frequently (e.g., every 4-6 hours) to increase the sense of danger.
- **Location Matters:** The type and frequency of hazards should vary depending on the party's location within [[the Mournland]]. Areas closer to the epicenter of the Mourning or to known sites of magical corruption should be more dangerous.
- **Weather Patterns:** You could establish certain weather patterns that increase the likelihood of specific hazards. For example, a "Croydonite Shard Storm" might be more common during a certain type of windstorm.
- **Difficulty Scaling:** Adjust the DCs and damage of the hazards based on the party's level.

**Mitigating the Risks:**

- **Finding Shelter:** Natural caves, ruined buildings, or other sheltered locations can offer some protection from the elements and reduce the chance of encountering a hazard.
- **Creating Camps:** As mentioned before, the party can attempt to create fortified camps. Success could reduce the frequency of hazard rolls, the DCs of saving throws, or the damage taken.
- **Protective Magic:** Spells like _protection from energy_, _Leomund's tiny hut_, _globe of invulnerability_, and _wind wall_ can provide temporary protection from some hazards.
- **Specialized Gear:**[[mournland]]-adapted gear, such as reinforced tents, goggles, or gas masks, could offer advantages against certain hazards.
- **Knowledge and Skills:** Characters with proficiency in Survival, Nature, or Arcana might be able to anticipate or mitigate the effects of some hazards.
- **Troxler's Innovations:** As Troxler advances in his crafting, he might be able to create items that offer protection from specific hazards (e.g., a "Croydonite Shielding Cloak" that grants resistance to fire damage and advantage on saving throws against Croydonite-related effects).

**The Calculated Risk:**

The goal is not to prevent the party from ever resting outside of settlements but to make it a strategic decision with potential consequences. Players should weigh the risks and rewards, considering factors like:

- **How far are they from a safe haven?**
- **How many resources do they have (hit points, spell slots, supplies)?**
- **What are the potential benefits of resting (regaining spell slots, crafting, research)?**
- **What are the known hazards of the area?**
- **Can they mitigate the risks through preparation or magic?**

**New Mechanics:**

- **Levels of Petrification:** Similar to exhaustion, but instead of dying at 5 failed saves they are turned into a crystal statue.
    - **Level 1:** Speed is halved.
    - **Level 2:** Disadvantage on attack rolls, saving throws, and ability checks.
    - **Level 3:** Petrified.
- **Teleportation Mishaps:** The unstable magic of [[the Mournland]]]] makes teleportation extremely dangerous. Any attempt to teleport into or within a hazardous area could have unpredictable and potentially catastrophic consequences.
- **Unspeakable Fate:** This could involve anything from instant death to being trapped in another dimension, transformed into a monster, or having one's soul devoured by a powerful entity. The key is to make it so horrifying that players will actively avoid triggering it.

**Remember:**

- **Telegraph the Danger:** Give players hints about the nature of these hazards before they encounter them. This could be through rumors, visual cues (e.g., the unnatural stillness of the air before a Shadow-Stealing Wind), or environmental storytelling (e.g., the crystal statues surrounding a Petrification Fog).
- **Offer Choices:** Even in the face of these dangers, players should still have agency. They might be able to find ways to avoid the hazards, mitigate their effects, or even turn them to their advantage.
- **Balance and Fairness:** While these hazards are meant to be dangerous, they shouldn't feel arbitrary or unfair. Make sure the DCs and damage are appropriate for the party's level, and provide opportunities for players to use their skills and abilities to overcome the challenges.
- **Narrative Impact:** These hazards should not only be mechanical challenges but also opportunities to reinforce the themes of horror, mystery, and the unpredictable nature of magic in your campaign.
