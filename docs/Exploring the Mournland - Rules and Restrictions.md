# **I. The Dangers of Long Resting in the Mournland (Early Game)**

- **Environmental Hazards:** Use the tables we created earlier (with additions as needed), but make them more frequent and more severe in the early game.
    - **Frequency:** Roll for hazards multiple times per night (e.g., every 2-4 hours).
    - **Severity:** Increase the DCs of saving throws, the damage dealt, or the duration of negative effects.
    - **Hazard Examples:**
        - **Croydonite Shard Storms:** More frequent, with larger and sharper shards.
        - **Spell Plague Winds:** Higher chance of triggering dangerous spell effects.
        - **Psychic Mist of the Far Realm:** Lower DC for saving throws, more severe psychic damage.
        - **Petrification Fog:** Faster-acting, requiring more frequent saving throws.
        - **Anti-Magic Tempest:** Affects lower-level spellcasters and magic items.
        - **Shadow-Stealing Winds:** Drain more hit points or ability scores in addition to their normal effects.
        - **The Screaming Silence:** As well as imposing disadvantage on initiative rolls, all creatures within the area have disadvantage on saving throws.
- **Interrupted Rests:** Make it very likely that long rests will be interrupted by hostile creatures, environmental hazards, or other events.
    - **High Interruption Chance:** Roll a d10 or d12 every hour. On a roll of 1-6, the rest is interrupted.
    - **Types of Interruptions:**
        - **Monster Attacks:** Attacks by mutated creatures, undead (like Conductor Husks), or even more dangerous entities.
        - **Environmental Hazards:** A sudden storm, a localized reality distortion, or a Croydonite bloom.
        - **Psychic Disturbances:** Nightmares, visions, or the whispers of trapped souls, causing psychic damage or levels of exhaustion.
- **No Hit Point Recovery:** As we established earlier, long rests in the Mournland do not restore hit points without a safe and well-made camp.
- **Exhaustion:** Emphasize the risks of exhaustion. Each level of exhaustion significantly weakens a character, making them more vulnerable to all the other dangers of the Mournland.
- **Corruption:** Consider adding a mechanic for "Mournland Corruption" that accumulates with each long rest spent in the wasteland. This could manifest as minor mutations, penalties to ability scores, or a vulnerability to certain types of damage. This could be tracked by adding a level of exhaustion that can only be removed by spending time in a safe haven, such as Salvation.

**II. Making Camps Safer**

- **Skill Checks:** Allow players to make skill checks to improve their campsites and reduce the risks of long resting:
    - **Survival:** To find a sheltered location, camouflage the camp, and identify potential dangers.
    - **Nature:** To identify and avoid areas with high concentrations of Croydonite or other hazards.
    - **Investigation:** To find signs of recent creature activity or to assess the stability of the terrain.
    - **Stealth:** To conceal the camp from prying eyes (both monstrous and humanoid).
- **Consequences of Failure:** If the skill checks fail, the camp might be more exposed to hazards, attract unwanted attention, or offer little protection.
- **Tools and Resources:** Certain tools and resources could provide bonuses to these skill checks:
    - **Tents:** (Initially unavailable) would provide a bonus to checks made to create a safe camp.
    - **Camouflage Nets:** (Initially unavailable) Could grant advantage on Stealth checks to conceal the camp.
    - **Alarm Spells/Devices:** Could provide early warning of approaching creatures.
    - **Purification Tablets:** Could be used to create a small area of purified ground, reducing the effects of Mournland Corruption.

**III. Unlocking Better Camping Gear**

- **Progression Through Town Tiers:** Tie the availability of better camping gear to the growth of Salvation:
    
    - **Tier 1 (Outpost):** No tents or specialized camping gear available. Players must rely on their skills and ingenuity to create makeshift shelters.
    - **Tier 2 (Frontier Town):** Basic tents become available (e.g., from "Old Man" Silas's store or a new merchant). These tents offer some protection from the elements and might reduce the chance of rest interruption, and allow the party to regain hit points on a long rest. They also reduce the chance of gaining a level of corruption, by one level.
    - **Tier 3 (Thriving Hub):** More durable and effective tents become available (e.g., from a specialized outfitter or a skilled artisan). These tents might provide bonuses to skill checks related to campsite creation, further reduce the chance of interruption, allow the party to regain hit dice on a long rest and offer resistance to certain environmental hazards, such as giving advantage on saving throws against extreme weather. They also reduce the chance of gaining a level of corruption by two levels.
    - **Tier 4 (Regional Power):** Advanced camping gear, potentially incorporating magic or specialized Mournland materials, becomes available. Examples include:
        - **Tents of Enduring Shelter:** These tents create a small, protected space where the effects of environmental hazards are lessened or negated. They also allow the party to regain hit points on a long rest, and prevent the party from gaining levels of corruption.
        - **Portable Campsite Fortifications:** These could include things like portable walls, alarm systems, or even magically enhanced guard animals.
        - **Croydonite-Resistant Gear:** Clothing or equipment that provides resistance to the negative effects of Croydonite exposure.
- **Crafting:** Troxler, or another skilled artisan, could eventually craft specialized camping gear using materials found in the Mournland. This could involve:
    
    - **Mutated Creature Hides:** Used to create tents that are resistant to specific types of damage or that blend in with the environment.
    - **Croydonite-Infused Fabric:** Used to create shelters that offer some protection against the corrupting influence of the Mournland (though with potential risks).
    - **Singing Stones:** Incorporated into a campsite to create a ward against certain types of creatures or to mask the sounds of the camp.

**IV. Narrative and Roleplaying**

- **Emphasize the Difficulty:** Use vivid descriptions to convey the harshness of the Mournland and the challenges of surviving outside of a safe haven. Describe the eerie silence, the unsettling landscapes, the strange weather patterns, and the constant sense of being watched.
- **Highlight the Risks:** Make sure players understand the risks involved in long resting in the Mournland. Emphasize the potential for exhaustion, corruption, and deadly encounters.
- **Create a Sense of Relief:** Make returning to Salvation feel like a true relief. Emphasize the warmth, safety, and comfort of the town in contrast to the dangers of the wasteland.
- **Reward Preparation:** Reward players who take the time to prepare for expeditions, scout out locations, and create well-fortified campsites.
- **Offer Choices:** Give players meaningful choices about where and how to rest. Do they press on to reach a specific location, or do they play it safe and set up camp early? Do they risk using Croydonite to enhance their shelter, or do they rely on more mundane methods?

By implementing these mechanics and narrative elements, you can make long resting in the Mournland a truly dangerous and meaningful activity in your West Marches campaign. You'll create a sense of tension and encourage players to be resourceful, strategic, and to value the safety and comfort of Salvation. This will also make the eventual acquisition of better camping gear feel like a significant accomplishment, a hard-won victory against the unforgiving landscape.

**I. Mounts and Transportation:**

- **No Ordinary Mounts:** Horses, mules, and other ordinary mounts cannot be used within the Mournland. The closer they get to the border, the more agitated and fearful they become. Beyond the northern edge of Salvation, they will outright refuse to proceed, driven mad by an instinctive terror.
- **Speak with Animals:** Using _speak with animals_ (or similar magic) on the terrified mounts reveals fragmented, fear-filled thoughts and images:
    - "The ground screams..."
    - "Whispers in the dust..."
    - "Broken sky... broken land..."
    - "It sees us... it _hungers_..."
    - "Turn back... turn back..."
    - "The wound... it bleeds..."
    - These messages hint at the corrupted nature of the land, the psychic residue of the Mourning, and potentially the influence of the entity trapped beneath the Glass Plateau.
- **Reason:** The animals are sensing the immense wrongness of the Mournland, the psychic scars of the Mourning, and the corrupting influence of Croydonite. Their instincts scream at them to flee. The animals are also picking up on the psychic echoes of the trapped entity beneath the Glass Plateau.
- **Alternatives:**
    - **Walking:** The primary mode of transportation will be on foot, making expeditions slower and more arduous. This will increase the time it takes the party to get to locations.
    - **Warforged Mounts:** a modified warforged, perhaps designed by House Cannith or created by Troxler using salvaged parts, might be able to withstand the Mournland's effects and serve as mounts. These would be rare and expensive. This could be something for the party to work towards.
    - **Magical Flight:** Spells like _fly_ or magic items that grant flight could be used, but these are typically limited in duration and might attract unwanted attention. Flying creatures are also affected by the fear, but to a lesser extent. Flying creatures will refuse to fly higher than 50 feet in the air.
    - **Other:** Perhaps there are creatures native to the Mournland that can be trained as mounts, or maybe the party can find a way to shield ordinary animals from the fear. This could involve a quest to find a specific plant, create a special harness, or learn a ritual that calms the animals' minds.

**II. The Effects of the Mournland:**

- **Environmental Hazards:** As established earlier, the Mournland is filled with dangerous and unpredictable environmental hazards, made more frequent and severe in the early game.
- **Corruption:** Prolonged exposure to the Mournland can cause physical and mental corruption. We can use the optional rule for tracking levels of corruption, where each level has an increasingly detrimental effect. Spending time in a safe haven, such as Salvation, for a day, will reduce a creature's corruption level by one.
- **Psychic Residue:** The Mourning left behind a psychic imprint on the land. This can manifest as:
    - **Whispers:** Faint, unsettling whispers that seem to come from nowhere, distracting spellcasters and potentially causing unease or fear. These whispers can be heard by all creatures, but are especially noticeable to those with high Wisdom or Charisma scores.
    - **Visions:** Fleeting, fragmented visions of the past, particularly the events of the Day of Mourning. These visions can be disturbing or even harmful, potentially causing psychic damage or inflicting conditions like frightened or stunned. These visions might be triggered by specific locations or events, or they might occur randomly.
    - **Emotional Imprints:** Areas where intense emotions were experienced during the Mourning might still radiate those emotions, affecting the moods and behaviors of those who enter them. For example, a battlefield might radiate anger and aggression, while a destroyed village might be filled with sorrow and despair. These emotions could be so strong that they force a creature to make a Wisdom saving throw or be forced to act in a certain way.
- **Unstable Magic:** Magic is unpredictable in the Mournland.
    - **Wild Magic Surges:** When spells are cast, there's a chance of triggering a Wild Magic Surge (you can use the table in the PHB or create your own custom table tailored to the Mournland).
    - **Spell Distortions:** Spells might have unintended side effects, their duration might be altered, or their area of effect might be warped.
    - **Dead Magic Zones:** Certain areas might be completely devoid of magic, suppressing all spellcasting and magical effects. These zones might shift or move unpredictably.

**III. Exploration Rules:**

- **Mapping:** Accurate mapping is crucial for survival. The party will need to keep track of their route, note landmarks, and mark any hazards they encounter.
- **Foraging:** As established, foraging for food and water is more difficult in the Mournland (higher DCs) and carries the risk of contamination.
- **Travel Time:** Travel times will be longer due to the lack of mounts and the difficult terrain. You might want to increase the base travel time per hex, especially in challenging terrain.
- **Resting:** Long resting is extremely dangerous outside of safe havens, with a high chance of interruption and no hit point recovery without a well-made camp.
- **Disease and Sickness:** The Mournland is a breeding ground for strange diseases and magical afflictions. Exposure to contaminated water, mutated creatures, or certain environmental hazards could result in illness. These diseases could be mundane or magical in nature, and they might require special treatments or spells to cure.

**IV. Making Exploration Rewarding:**

- **Unique Discoveries:** Despite the dangers, the Mournland offers the potential for unique discoveries: lost artifacts, forgotten knowledge, rare materials, and encounters with strange and wondrous creatures.
- **Salvage:** The ruins of Cyre are filled with salvage, from mundane items to valuable components to powerful magical artifacts.
- **Croydonite:** The party will be able to find and harvest Croydonite, though they will need to learn how to handle it safely and deal with its ethical implications.
- **Reputation:** Successfully exploring the Mournland and returning with valuable information or resources will increase the party's reputation in Salvation and potentially attract the attention of powerful factions.
- **Personal Growth:** Overcoming the challenges of the Mournland will make the characters stronger, more resilient, and more knowledgeable about the world around them.

**By establishing these rules and restrictions, you create a challenging and immersive experience for your players. The Mournland becomes a place of both great danger and great opportunity, where every expedition is a risk and every discovery is hard-earned. The inability to use mounts will force players to be more cautious, to plan their routes carefully, and to rely on their own skills and ingenuity to survive. It also emphasizes the unnatural and unsettling nature of the Mournland, making it a truly unique and memorable setting.**
